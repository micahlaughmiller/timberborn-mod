# Hydrology: droughts, badtides, dams, canals

- Badtides do not start immediately: around cycle 4 or 5 on Normal, cycle 3 on Hard.
- Badwater kills crops and trees up to about 7 tiles from the water's edge, and sickens beavers. Keep bypass
  channels 7 tiles from farms and groves, or wall them with levees.
- A standard dam holds water to about 0.65 of a block and lets the rest overflow. A levee blocks flow completely.
  Water does not flow diagonally through solid blocks.
- Dam before the first drought across a river choke point so a reservoir stays upstream. The Dam is available from
  the start (20 logs per block, per the game's data).
- Store drinking water in tanks, not in open river: a single open water tile holds little, and open surfaces
  evaporate. Evaporation scales with exposed surface area, so prefer deep and narrow reservoirs, and irrigation
  canals about 2 to 3 tiles wide (narrower dries out, wider wastes water).
- Badtide plan, before metal: a two-channel split at an upstream choke point. Floodgates on the colony intake and on
  a bypass channel, with levees around the bypass. When a badtide is announced, raise the colony gate and open the
  bypass so badwater leaves the map; keep the colony gate lowered in clean periods. Floodgates need science first.
- Once metal is available, sluices (bottom-level discharge, can hold a target downstream depth) automate this:
  temperate = colony intake open, bypass closed; badtide = intake closed, bypass open; drought = both closed to keep
  the reservoir.
- Badwater is useful late: centrifuges turn it into Extract, which feeds dynamite. A sealed badwater basin, far from
  crops, can keep it for that instead of venting everything off the map.
- Isolated farmland far from the river can be kept green with a Fluid Dump in a dug 3x3 hollow.
